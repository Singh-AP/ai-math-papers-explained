## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the paper:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- "The object" panel: both graphs are garbled. The y-axis labels are out of order (1/2 sits below 1/4), the x-axis labels ("1/2ʳ", "k/4ʳ", "3/8ʳ") mean nothing, the drawn graphs have flat pieces and a jump, and the slope labels do not match the segments. Elements of F are continuous and strictly increasing, with slopes that are integer powers of 2 (such as 1/2, 1 and 2). See the hand-made [generators figure](figures/generators-A-B.svg) for the real A and B.
- "Følner" is misspelled three times ("Falner", "Foiner", "Fplner").
- The bell curve labelled "translation-invariant average" is only decoration. Neither Z nor any infinite group has a translation-invariant probability distribution on its elements.
- "The result" panel: the formula reads "max |A| ≥ c > 0". It should be $\max_{h\in S} \lvert hA\triangle A\rvert/\lvert A\rvert \ge c > 0$.
- The right-hand "engine" panel repeats the "Step 1" caption, labels both halves of the interval "1/2", and garbles a formula as "f(m) − xf". Its binary tree suggests the interval is split in halves; the paper uses D separated parent intervals, each with D descendants.
- "Status & verification" calls the result "machine-verified in Lean 4 (ThompsonNonamenability.lean)". openai/math lists the statement as formalized and checkable with Comparator. That file holds the challenge statement; the proof is under `lean/OAI/GroupTheory/Thompson/`. The catalogue's review field reads `unchecked`, and this explainer did not re-run the build.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- It is a selection. It skips, among others, Day (1949, 1957), Følner (1955), Ol'shanskii's 1980 counterexample, Brown–Geoghegan (1984) and Moore's 2013 results. See the [history table](../README.md#2-a-short-history).
- The 2009–2021 panel is titled "Epoch of Claims and Retractions" and puts red crosses on the Akhmedov and Shavgulidze cards, which suggests the claims were all retracted. Only Akhmedov's 2009 preprint and Moore's 2012 preprint were withdrawn. Moore identified errors in Shavgulidze's approach, and the paper describes Akhmedov's 2021 version only as a claim.
- "Sept 2026: Lean 4 formal verification … Result formally verified in Lean 4" overstates the documentation, which lists the statement as formalized, gives no date for the formalization, and has the review field `unchecked`. Likewise "OpenAI Proves F is Nonamenable" and "defying classification until 2026" present an unreviewed preprint as settled.
- Decorations such as the "π" icon next to von Neumann and the scribbles on Thompson's notes are meaningless. The cartoon figures are generic, not portraits.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- Slide 1: the subtitle calls the result "machine-verified" (see the Lean note above), and the footer appears twice, once with an unbalanced parenthesis.
- Slide 2: the word "completely" is duplicated. The paper's setting is discrete groups, not general topological groups.
- Slide 4: "Boundary: Exponentially Expands (Dwarfs Interior)" exaggerates. For balls in F₂ the ratio tends to 1, so the part that moves is about as large as the ball itself, not vastly larger.
- Slide 5: the "1949 von Neumann–Day question" card appears twice, the second time with the typo "nonamable". 1949 is the year Day coined the word "amenable"; the question's first written appearance is Day's 1957 paper.
- Slide 6: the example graph goes **down** twice, so it is not an element of F. Elements of F are strictly increasing, and every slope is an integer power of 2. The phrase "generated by a rigid set of dyadic bisections" is vague; F is generated by the two maps A and B.
- Slide 7: "faulty proofs … were published and retracted" overstates the history (see the timeline erratum above). "F₋₂" should read "F₂".
- Slide 8: "Published by OpenAI" means released as a preprint; it has not been peer reviewed.
- Slide 10: it says each grid's colour averages "the colors of its descendants". In the paper, $p(T)$ is $f$ applied to the average of the colours of the grid's normalized restrictions $T_{I_1},\dots,T_{I_D}$ to the **parent** intervals, and $p(T) = 0$ when $T$ fails to respect a parent. Descendants enter only in the analysis ($X_{I_i} = f(z_i)$). The grids are dyadic partitions, usually uneven, not only uniform ones.
- Slide 11: "Group F can carry any separated pair of dyadic intervals onto any other" drops the hypotheses of Lemma 2.2. The intervals must be basic dyadic intervals with both endpoints inside (0,1). Every element of F fixes 0 and 1, so an interval starting at 0 can only go to another interval starting at 0. "Perfectly distributing the geometric friction" has no meaning in the paper.
- Slide 13: both corollaries rely on companion theorems from other openai/math families (251 and 214), which the slide leaves out.
- Slide 14: the Lean code is **invented**. The real statement is `OAI.ThompsonNonamenability.thompson_F_nonamenable_composition` (see [section 3 of the explainer](../README.md#3-what-the-paper-proves)). The slide also labels the proof "Human" ingenuity, but the paper was produced by an internal OpenAI model, and "a century of conjecture" is wrong: Geoghegan's conjecture dates from 1979. "Complete formal verification" overstates the documentation, as above.
- Slide 15: "fully verified" overstates the Lean status. "Proving once and for all that the free group F₂ is not the sole architect of mathematical paradoxes" is not new: Ol'shanskii's 1980 counterexamples already showed that nonamenability does not need a free subgroup.
- The proposed fix for slides 5, 6, 10 and 14 was **not** applied, because the shared NotebookLM quota was below the threshold for a revision.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Its proof walk-through (sections 3–6) follows the paper closely and is accurate, apart from the points on sections 4.1 and 5.2 below.
- Section 4.1 states Lemma 1.2 as if it held in any real Hilbert space ("In a real Hilbert space H … there exists"). The paper only says that some real Hilbert space carries such a map. By Brouwer's fixed-point theorem it must be infinite-dimensional.
- Section 2.1 attributes the free-subgroup obstruction to "the classical Tits-alternative paradox". The obstruction is von Neumann's observation that a group containing a free subgroup of rank 2 is not amenable. The Tits alternative is a theorem about linear groups.
- Section 2.1's Brown–Geoghegan bullet garbles their title ("an infinite-dimensional torsion-free classifying space"). In their title it is F that is infinite-dimensional and torsion-free. The sentence "homologically, F behaves like a finite-dimensional object, ruling out simple infinite-dimensional topological obstructions" is not in the paper.
- Section 5.2 says $G(x) = x$ "smoothly" along the negative tail of the curve. $G$ is only Lipschitz, and that remark is the report's own (correct) observation rather than the paper's.
- Section 7.1 says the result "has been formally verified" and is "formalized in `ThompsonNonamenability.lean`". See the Lean note under the overview infographic.
