## Errata

NotebookLM gets the big picture of this paper right: the parity mechanism, the handshake lemma, the two safeguards and the Gardam comparison all appear correctly. It also overstates the verification status in several places and garbles some pictures and text. The issues below were found by reading every slide, both infographics and the report against the paper.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`** (15 slides, not revised)
- Slide 2: the left box says an "AI-generated, machine-checked paper entirely disproved it". The paper disproves the conjecture over $\mathbb{F}_2$ only, and openai/math lists its main theorem as *formalized in Lean* with the catalogue review field `unchecked`. The theorem's annotation labels are attached to the wrong parts: "Ring of Coefficients" points at $\alpha,\beta$, "Zero Divisor Condition" at $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$, and "Group Properties" at $\alpha\beta = 0$. The line `import TorsionFreeZeroDivisors.lean` is not valid Lean.
- Slides 2 and 4: the "LEAN FORMALIZED / MACHINE CHECKED" badge is decoration. On slide 4 it sits next to the torsion identity, which is not part of the formalization.
- Slide 5: "Disproved (OpenAI, 2026)" for the zero-divisor conjecture means over $\mathbb{F}_2$, in a preprint. The unit-conjecture row's "where $a, b$ are not just trivial group elements" is loose: the trivial units are $\lambda g$.
- Slide 6: the "Structural Map of the 21-Term Unit" network is invented decoration, not Gardam's unit.
- Slide 8: the extra letters are drawn as "A B C", which clashes with the vertex sets $A$ and $B$. In the paper they are three unnamed extra letters, written $e_1, e_2, e_3$ in the explainer.
- Slide 9: it only says that two distinct lines meet in one point. The paper's odd overlap also uses the equal-line case ($`q + 1 = 129`$ shared points) and the extra letters (0 or 2 shared).
- Slide 10: the picture is wrong. It draws a bipartite graph between "Set A" and "Set B" with the terms $g_x h_y^{-1}$ on the edges. In the paper, the vertices of the simultaneous-step graph are the *pairs* $(x,y)$, each carrying the term $g_x h_y^{-1}$, and a step joins $(x,y)$ to $(xt, yt)$. Box 3 has the typo "termsels out to 0" (it should read "terms cancel out to 0").
- Slide 11: the graph components are drawn as triangles. The paper's graphs have girth at least $L = \lfloor c\log n\rfloor$, so they have no short cycles.
- Slide 12: the "The Danger" box appears twice. Both roots are drawn inside one graph, whereas $x_A$ lies in $\Gamma_A$ and $x_B$ in the separate graph $\Gamma_B$. "Topological path routing guarantees" is vague: the paper proves root separation by excluding reduced spherical arrangements (Proposition 5.1).
- Slide 13: "Strictly Torsion-Free Space" is loose wording. Asphericity of $X$ makes $X$ a finite $K(G,1)$, and that makes the *group* torsion-free.
- Slide 14: "Theorem 1.1 Verified" overstates the status (preprint; main theorem listed as formalized in Lean).
- Slide 15: the Lean code is invented and garbled. Its first line, `∀ G … [TorsionFree G], ZeroDivisors(ℤ[G]) → False`, states the *conjecture* (and over $`\mathbb{Z}`$), which is the opposite of the formalized theorem. The real statement is an existence statement over `MonoidAlgebra (ZMod 2) G`; see section 3 of the explainer. The chalkboard text is gibberish, "verified entirely in Lean" overstates the status, and the text has the typo "are are".

A single revision of slides 2, 10, 12, 14 and 15 was prepared but not run, because less than 35% of the rolling NotebookLM quota remained. The command is recorded in the work directory as `slides-revise-command.sh`.

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- Panel 3: "Nonontrivial Unit" is a typo for "Nontrivial Unit".
- Panel 4: "Gardam's unit disproved the top rung" contradicts Panel 3's own ladder, which puts the unit at the bottom. In the explainer's terms, a nontrivial unit is the lowest, weakest rung, and Gardam's result leaves the zero-divisor rung unbroken (which the panel does say).
- Panel 7: the step diagram mixes the two graphs' vertices $x$, $xt$, $y$, $yt$ into one picture. The paper's steps act on pairs: $(x,y) \to (xt, yt)$. "$\mathbb{P}_2$" is a typo for $\mathbb{F}_2$ (also in Panel 9).
- Panel 9: "Fully Machine-Checked in Lean" and "formalized and verified" overstate the status. openai/math lists the main theorem as formalized, with the catalogue review field `unchecked`, and this explainer did not re-run the build.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- "2021–2026: Counterexamples and Final Resolution" overstates things. Only characteristic 2 is settled; the zero-divisor conjecture over $\mathbb{C}$ and over $\mathbb{F}_p$ for odd $p$ remains open.
- "Early 2026: Fisher and Sánchez-Peralta established the zero-divisor property for 3-manifold groups." The result is for *torsion-free* 3-manifold groups, and "early 2026" is **unverified** (the paper's bibliography gives only the year).
- Gardam's unit formula is garbled ("$(2_2 \times 1 - \dots)$"), and the formula scraps around the magnifying glass are decoration.
- The summary bubble's "modern algorithmic disproofs" is loose. Gardam's unit was found by a SAT-solver search, but this paper's proof is a probabilistic existence proof.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 2, the array: the arrow "No Zero Divisors ⟸ No Nontrivial Idempotents" points the wrong way. Having no zero divisors implies having no nontrivial idempotents, not the converse.
- Section 3, the table: the "Unique Product Property" row lists Rips–Segev, Steenbock and Mian–Siddique as contributors to a positive result. Their work is about groups *without* unique products. "Unilinear term" is garbled.
- Section 5.2: the ordinary-letter bound is printed as $q/(q+1)^2 + \dots$. The paper has $q/(q+1) + 3p^2 + 12/(q+1)^2$.
- Section 5.3: the switching-lemma sentence garbles the statement. The paper bounds the conditional probability of an extra edge by $1/(np - s - r_n)$.
- Section 1, "Geometric approaches": the description of Mineyev's work as "paper-folding analogies on 2-complexes", and the claim that these obstructions "motivated" the paper's method, are not in the paper. The paper says only that its construction "supplies its own coefficient cancellation, root separation, and torsion-freeness proofs rather than applying those geometric criteria".
- Section 2: Farrell–Jones is not why $K[P]$ has no zero divisors. Gardam says the zero-divisor conjecture is known for $P$ because it is virtually abelian.
- Section 7: "The proof has been machine-checked in the Lean 4 proof assistant" overstates the status (see the slide 14 and Panel 9 notes). "A finitely presented torsion-free group algebra" should read "the group algebra over $\mathbb{F}_2$ of a finitely presented torsion-free group".
