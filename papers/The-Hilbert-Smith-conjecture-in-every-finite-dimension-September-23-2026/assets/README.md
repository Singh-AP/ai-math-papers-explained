## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the paper:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- Typos: "locally compest", "fuithfully", "linita-dimensional" (finite-dimensional), "nasted", and "September 2028" (the preprint is dated 23 September **2026**).
- The $`\mathbb{Z}_p`$ panel garbles the subgroups: "$`p^e\mathbb{Z}_g`$ ($`8\mathbb{Z}_g, 9\mathbb{Z}_p, 27\mathbb{Z}_a`$)" and the tree labels "$`3\mathbb{Z}_a`$", "$`3\mathbb{Z}_0`$" should read $`p^k\mathbb{Z}_p`$ and $`3\mathbb{Z}_3, 9\mathbb{Z}_3, 27\mathbb{Z}_3`$. The circle is written "$`2^\varepsilon`$" instead of $`S^1`$.
- The mechanism panel says "$`p^6`$ separate covering sheets" and "$`\zeta^7`$ on sheet $`/`$"; it should be $`p^k`$ sheets and $`\zeta^j`$ on sheet $`j`$. It also says that "an odd-sphere homotopy map $`D_2`$ concentrates a degree-one test into $`V`$". In the paper (Proposition 5.8), it is $`D_s\circ a`$, a degree-$`s`$ multiple of the degree-one test for some positive $`s`$, that is homotopic to a test concentrated over $`V`$.
- The status panel puts "Dims 1–3" under John Pardon. Dimensions 1 and 2 are classical (Montgomery–Zippin); Pardon proved dimension 3 in 2013. The headline "Proved in All Dimensions by AI" and the "Proved in EVERY Finite Dimension" badge state as settled what is an unrefereed, unformalized preprint (the panel text itself says "unrefereed"). The "Limited Scope" box is unexplained.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- The 1960–1999 caption, "Yang, Repovš–Ščepin, Maleshich, and Martin settle the conjecture for smooth, Lipschitz, Hölder, and quasiconformal actions", misattributes two results. Yang (1960) proved the dimension jump of the orbit space ($`n+2`$, per Pardon), not a regularity case. The smooth (differentiable, $`C^2`$) case is Bochner–Montgomery (1946), who are missing from the timeline.
- Newman's theorem (1931) and Paul A. Smith (1941) are missing.
- "1952–1953: … Gleason, Montgomery, Zippin, and Yamabe prove locally Euclidean groups are Lie groups" folds Yamabe into the 1952 result; Yamabe's 1953 papers extended the structure theory to all locally compact groups.
- Typos: "sheof Witt theory" (sheaf), "Mortgomery Zypin" on the book cover, "smoothy".
- The portraits are AI-generated likenesses, not photographs of the people named.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 1: "any locally Euclidean topological group (and more generally, general locally compact groups via Yamabe's structure theory) is a Lie group" is wrong as written. Not every locally compact group is a Lie group ($`\mathbb{Z}_p`$ is not). Yamabe extended the *structure theory* to locally compact groups.
- Section 1: "for the group $`G`$, it prevents uncountably large continuous pathologies" is NotebookLM's own gloss on second countability, not the paper's.
- Section 1: the orientation-preserving part of the chart reduction is attributed to Pardon. Pardon's §4.1 gives the faithful action on a chart subset; the paper adds orientation preservation by passing to $`G = 2H`$ (Proposition 5.1).
- Section 2: "$`n \to n+3`$ with field coefficients" is not in the paper. The paper only says that Raymond "emphasizes the different behavior with field coefficients"; Yang's jump, per Pardon, is to integral cohomological dimension $`n+2`$.
- Section 3: "resolves a long-standing dynamical conjecture" overstates: it is Pardon's Conjecture 1.4 (2013).
- Section 4: $`\mathcal{T}_0(X)`$ is described as a "thick subcategory". In the paper $`\mathcal{T}^0(X)`$ is the smallest full replete *triangulated* subcategory containing the generators, and $`\mathcal{T}(X)`$ is its closure under summands (which is thick).
- Section 4.5: the mapping telescope is the paper's construction (the same argument appears in Dranishnikov–Ferry–Weinberger), built using Serre's theorems; calling it "Serre's odd-sphere mapping telescope" is loose.
- Section 4.6: "integral classes $`z_i(a)`$, which remain elements of the fixed integral lattice $`L_d^{-1}\mathbb{Z}u_d`$" conflates a class with its rational image. The classes live in $`E_d(S^d)`$; their **rational images** lie in the lattice. Likewise $`[A_a,\alpha_a] = 2u_d`$ holds for the rational image.
- Section 5: "requires no boundary conditions" misreads the paper. The manifold must be **without boundary**; what the paper does not need is a regularity assumption on the boundary of the chart subset $`O`$.
- Checked and correct: the exact statements of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, the exactness sign $`\delta_r = (-1)^r`$ and the Koszul pairing form, the cylinder triangle $`J \to H \to E`$, the dense-doubling formula $`D(q) = q \perp q \perp H(x[1])`$, the Proposition 6.4 isometry, the condition in Proposition 6.7, and the final arithmetic.

**`mindmaps.md` / `mindmap-proof.json`**
- "Reduces general conjecture to p-adic case" is listed under Theorem 1.2. Theorem 1.2 *is* the $`p`$-adic case; the reduction is the classical one used in the proof of Theorem 1.1.
- "Integral image in L_d^(-1) Z ud" and "Each z_i(a) = (4/p^k) ud in fixed lattice" should refer to rational images. The second node reads as a fact, but it is the statement that leads to the contradiction once $`p^k > 4L_d`$.
