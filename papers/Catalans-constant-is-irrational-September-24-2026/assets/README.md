## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the paper:

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`** (the shipped, revised deck)
- The first deck had four clearly wrong slides, and all four were regenerated once with `nlm slides revise`. A slide-by-slide diff confirmed that no other slide changed. The fixes were:
  - Slide 5's Fourier column said "Denominators grow by: n! (Massive)" next to "Success", which contradicted the slide's own logic. It now says that $n!$ makes the first $n+1$ terms integers and leaves a tail between $0$ and $1/n$.
  - Slide 6 showed invented Lean code (`import Mathlib.NumberTheory.Catalan`, `theorem catalan_constant_irrational`, made-up `have` steps) and called the preprint "strictly formalized and verified". It now shows the real Comparator statement `OAI.InternalCatalan.catalan_irrational`.
  - Slide 9 stated the floor $\mathcal{L}_N > -2.29084$ unconditionally ("it cannot physically be smaller"). The floor holds only in the limit, and only if $G$ were rational.
  - Slide 11 stated the ceiling as $\mathcal{L}_N < -2.290939875$ for every $N$ ("it cannot physically be larger"). The paper proves only $\limsup \mathcal{L}_N \le -2.290939875$, and our computed $\mathcal{L}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{L}_4$ all lie above that value.
- Slide 1: the subtitle calls the problem "an unsolved mathematical mystery", although the paper claims to solve it.
- Slide 3: "spanning trees on grid graphs" comes from the Wikipedia background source, not from the paper. The drawing is a decorative tangle, not the Whitehead link.
- Slide 4: the words "ruins the" are duplicated, and "prime denominators" should read "denominators".
- Slide 5: the Zudilin column ("Fast Decay", "Faster Growth") is a paraphrase. The paper gives no rates.
- Slide 6: the title still says "AI-Assisted", but the paper was produced by an AI model. The rendered Lean statement has an unbalanced parenthesis.
- Slide 8: "The Chebyshev Filter" mixes up two things. In the paper the "filter" is the integer column filter $s^{b+k}(1-s)^q$, and the Chebyshev rows cancel $\zeta(2)$ through Taylor contact. The drawing also leaves $\zeta(2)$ in the top rows, which suggests the cancellation is partial.
- Slide 9: the prime "pyramid" is decoration (it repeats primes), and the revised text runs into the bottom of its frame.
- Slide 10: "never zero along the prime sequence" overstates Proposition 4.1, which gives $\Delta_p \ne 0$ for every sufficiently large prime $p$, under the rationality hypothesis.
- Slide 11: the revision introduced a typo, "Cauchy's double alternate" for "alternant". The slide also skips the interpolation, Hadamard and energy steps.
- Slide 12: the "~0.0001" overlap uses the paper's rounded constants. The exact gap is $0.00013$.
- Slide 13: "a 150-year-old number theory mystery" is unverified. "The topology of our universe" is hyperbole, since these manifolds are mathematical objects. "Definitively irrational" overstates the status of a preprint. The drawing is not the Whitehead link.
- Slide 14: "Proof fully formalized in Lean 4" goes beyond what we checked. The scope document says the formalization proves the theorem, but `lean/formalization.yaml` does not list it, and this explainer did not re-run the build. The slide also calls $\zeta(5)$ an "even beta value".
- Slide 15: the formula in the middle panel is decorative nonsense.

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- Panel 1 draws the curve rising from the origin, like $\arctan x$. The function $y = \arctan(x)/x$ actually falls from $1$ to $\pi/4$, as on slide 1 and in our figure.
- Panel 3, "The Test", repeats panel 2 (padlock and scales). The test itself appears in panel 5, which is mislabeled "The Result: G is Irrational!".
- Panel 4: a banner reading "Definitive proof established… 2026 breakthrough paper from OpenAI" overlaps and hides the sentence "Clearing denominators multiplied a…".
- Panel 6: the matrix entries are invented formulas, not the paper's entries. The floor and ceiling ($\mathcal{L}_N > -2.29084$, $\mathcal{L}_N < -2.2909$) appear without "in the limit", and the floor appears without "only if $G$ were rational".
- Bottom panel: "β(4) or ζ(8)" should read "β(4) or ζ(5)", since $\zeta(8) = \pi^8/9450$ is known. "Lean 4 Verification" overstates the status in the same way as slide 14.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- The introduction says "OpenAI researchers finalized its proof". The paper was produced by an internal OpenAI model.
- "1865: Eugène Catalan Defines G": what Wikipedia records is that Catalan published a memoir with fast series for the constant, which is named after him. That he "defined" it is unverified.
- "Formally Proven Irrational" (twice) overstates the verification status (see slide 14).
- The "“ζ(3)” ≤ bounds" label and the matrix `detm = …` are meaningless decorations. The portraits are generic drawings, not likenesses.
- Many events from the prompt are missing (Beukers 1979, Zudilin 2003/2017/2019, Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang 2024). That is not an error, but the timeline is far from complete.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 1: the claim that the question "remained an open conjecture for over a century" is not in the paper and is unverified. The Euler-number formula for $\beta(2k+1)$ and the transcendence remark (via Lindemann) are not in the paper either, but they are correct.
- Section 2: the phrase "$q_m \in \mathbb{N}$ are non-integer linear form denominators" is garbled.
- Section 2, "Classical Successes", attaches the decay rate $(\sqrt2-1)^4 \approx 0.0294$ to Beukers' $\zeta(2)$ integral, but that rate belongs to $\zeta(3)$. For $\zeta(2)$ it is $((\sqrt5-1)/2)^5 \approx 0.090$ (general knowledge), and $0.090 \times e^2 \approx 0.67 < 1$, so the conclusion survives.
- Section 2 says that for $G$ the cleared forms "explode" because denominators grow like $e^{cm}$ with $c > r$. The paper says only that Zudilin's integer linear forms do not tend to zero.
- Section 2 table:
  - The determinant row says the arithmetic "lower[s] denominator cost to $-2.29084$, enabling analytical decay ($-2.29094$)". Both numbers are bounds on $\mathcal{L}_N$, not costs, and $-2.29094$ is stronger than the paper's $-2.290939875$.
  - "Individual linear form costs remain strictly above 1" (the Padé row) is not in the paper.
- Section 3, Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang: the report puts the extra $\pi^2$ term "for conductor 3", and its parenthetical contrasting it with the present proof is wrong. The paper says the extra $\pi^2$ term appears in the real identity related to Calegari's 2-adic Catalan analogue, that is, conductor 4.
- Section 4: "solves longstanding conjectures regarding hyperbolic volumes" is not in the paper. Where the report says the minimum is "realized uniquely" by the two link complements, the paper says only "attained by".
- Section 5, Step 1: the $M(i,j)$ integrand is written $\lvert t\rvert f(t)$. It should be $\lvert t\rvert/f(t)$.
- Section 5, Step 5:
  - The formula for $i'$ is mis-parenthesized. The paper has $i' = (i+1+d-\ell)/p - 1$.
  - "Column Pairing (Lemma 3.2)" is mislabeled. The pairing is in the text of Section 3.2, and Lemma 3.2 is the central-layer lemma.
- Section 5, Step 8: the displayed integral puts $\prod \lvert t_i\rvert^{C-1}(1-t_i)^h$ in the denominator. In the paper this factor multiplies the integrand.
- Section 5, Step 10: "50 candidate bracket endpoints" is imprecise. The paper's Table 2 lists fifty point values at the candidate points: the stationary-point brackets and the division points.
- Section 7 does not say that the paper was produced by an AI model, or that `lean/formalization.yaml` does not list the formalization.
- Apart from these points, the step-by-step section matches the paper: the parameters, $505/4608$, $8609/2$, $-2.2908095\ldots$, $-2.290939875$, $v_p(\Delta_p) = -96p$ and the block factorization.
