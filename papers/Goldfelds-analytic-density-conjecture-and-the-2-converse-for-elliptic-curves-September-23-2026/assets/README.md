## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the two papers:

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- Slide 1: the corner logo reads "GOLEFELD RESOLUTION" (typo). The subtitle's "AI-produced proofs" are unrefereed preprints.
- Slide 2: the right-hand plot overlays many curves, and one of them has a crossing point at $x = 1$. That curve is singular, so it is not an elliptic curve. The picture is decoration only: a twist $dy^2 = x^3 + ax + b$ is a single curve, stretched vertically by $1/\sqrt{d}$ when $d > 0$.
- Slide 3: "Each sign occurs for exactly half of all twists" holds only in the limit (density 1/2), not exactly at every height.
- Slide 6: **wrong.** The top box says the "Poonen–Rains random model & Smith (2025) capture the 2-Selmer rank distribution (half 0, half 1)". The Poonen–Rains model is a model for 2-Selmer groups, whose ranks are *not* half 0 and half 1 (see section 1.6 of the explainer). The half-and-half statement is Smith's theorem about the $2^\infty$-Selmer *corank*. Smith's abstract notes that in new cases his 2-Selmer distributions differ from the Poonen–Rains model. The side text blames the over-count on "divisible 2-primary contributions" of Ш. In fact the 2-Selmer rank over-counts because of the *finite* 2-torsion of Ш; the divisible part is what $c_2$ itself measures.
- Slide 8: correct in substance. Its last sentence is loose. Lemma 2.4, a Cassels–Tate pairing argument, already gives $c_2 = d_2$ and the vanishing of the finite part of $`\mathrm{Sha}[2^\infty]`$. What is new is the analytic rank and the finiteness of the whole of Ш.
- Slide 9: the left label "Smith (2025): 2-Selmer coranks" should read "$`2^\infty`$-Selmer coranks". The bell curve is misleading: the distribution is two values (0 and 1) with probability 1/2 each.
- Slide 10: the hypercube drawing highlights **two** orange "zero" vertices, and the two callouts point to different ones. The cube has exactly one zero vertex, the curve $E$ itself (compare the hand-made [cube figure](figures/missing-vertex-cube.png)). Step 3 should say the tests succeed at *every* nonzero vertex.
- Slide 11: the label "Heegner power series (Rank 1)" appears twice. The bottom sentence matches the paper.
- Slide 12: the histogram is schematic. Bars over $[0,1]$ and $[1,2]$ stand for ranks 0 and 1, and the curve beyond 2 is not data.
- Slide 13: "Theorem 1.1 and 1.2 describe an asymptotic limit" is wrong for Theorem 1.1, which is a statement about each single curve. The limits are Theorem 1.2 and the companion's mean theorem. The numbers (44.5%, 49.9%, 5.6%, mean 0.613) are this explainer's PARI/GP computation, not results from the papers.
- Slide 14: "resolved for all rational curves" means elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$, and it is a preprint claim. "Densities are exactly 50/50" holds in the limit. "uncertified uncertified" is a duplicated word.

The overlapping circles are decoration; the three ranks are numbers, not sets.

- "Main Results" panel: the headline "Mean Analytic Rank = 1/2" is a limit (the mean *tends to* 1/2), as the supporting text says.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- Title typo: "Conjectur::".
- "1986–2004: Low-Rank Converses & Modularity" is mislabelled. Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin proved the *forward* implication (analytic rank 0 or 1 gives rank 0 or 1 and finite Ш). The converse is what these papers prove.
- "2008–2019: Random models and statistics yield positive proportions" conflates two things. The random models (Poonen–Rains) and Selmer statistics (Swinnerton-Dyer, Kane, Smith 2017) concern Selmer groups. The positive proportions of analytic ranks 0 and 1 for special curves (Vatsal 1998, Kriz–Li 2019) came from other methods. Vatsal's result is from 1998, before this box starts.
- "2026: OpenAI releases establish…" presents unrefereed preprints as established. The "Selmer converses" are the family-002 paper.
- The bell curve centred at 1/2 is misleading: the predicted distribution is ranks 0 and 1 with probability 1/2 each, not a bell curve.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 2 table: "Proves Goldfeld's original 1979 Conjecture (B)" should add "in the signed squarefree counting convention". Goldfeld counted discriminants of quadratic fields, and the companion makes this distinction itself.
- Section 3 history:
  - Vatsal's $X_0(19)$ result is dated 2004; it is from 1998 (Math. Ann. 311). The phrase "using modular symbols and $p$-adic $L$-functions" is not from the papers and was not checked.
  - Swinnerton-Dyer and Kane are put in the "2000s". Kane's paper is from 2013, and both need *full* rational 2-torsion, not just "rational 2-torsion".
  - **Wrong:** the Poonen–Rains (2012) model is said to predict "$`p`$-Selmer coranks 0 and 1 each with density 1/2". It models $p$-Selmer groups, which are finite, and predicts a spread-out distribution of their ranks.
  - Tian 2014 is credited with "genus-period congruences"; those are Tian–Yuan–Zhang (2017).
  - **Wrong date:** the unrestricted Smith theorem for every curve is placed in "2020/2022". It is his 2025 preprint (arXiv:2503.17619); the 2022 papers treated further residual cases.
  - "Unramified reducible case of Lemma 2.3" is not the paper's wording.
- Section 6, companion mechanics:
  - Lemma 4.1 is described as expressing derivatives "as integral transformations of automorphic forms over GL(2)". It is a contour-integral identity for one twisted $L$-function: a weighted Dirichlet series equals $\ell^{-k}g_d^{(k)}(0)$, so it vanishes when the rank exceeds $k$ with the matching sign.
  - **Invented formula:** the displayed asymptotic $\sum L(E(d),1)M(1,\chi_d)^2 \sim CX$ is not in the companion. The companion proves *unmollified* second-moment bounds with powers of $\log X$, and mean-square bounds for mollified pieces in an independent random model.
  - The Jensen bound uses a disk around the point 2 (radius 5/2 in the companion's normalization, which contains the centre), not "small disks centred at $s = 1$".
- Section 7: "the full BSD conjecture holds up to 2-power valuations for 100% of twists" garbles the two-primary companion's Corollary 16.1. That corollary is the exact *2-part* of the BSD formula on a density-one set. (The full formula at every prime on a density-one set is the family-002 result.) The "family 002 thematic context" bullet is vague and links ideas the papers do not link.
- **Wrong**, section 8: "For a specific twist with $c_2 = 2$, the theorem does not resolve whether $a = 2$ or $a = 0$." Analytic rank 0 is impossible there, because Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin (Lemma 2.1) would force $c_2 = 0$. Such a twist has analytic rank at least 2, and what is open is whether it equals 2 and equals the rank.
- Section 9 table:
  - **Swapped:** "Companion Theorem 1.1" is listed as the tail estimate and "Theorem 1.2" as the mean theorem and moments. In the companion, Theorem 1.1 is the mean, Theorem 1.2 is the tail, and the algebraic-rank moments are Corollary 1.3.
  - Lemma 4.1 is again described through "automorphic forms".
  - Several one-line descriptions of Lemmas 5.x–9.x paraphrase lemma titles and were not checked individually.
- Checked and correct: the theorem statements in section 4, the index-comparison formula of Proposition 3.2, the matrix correction $M_{qp} = B_{qp} - L(\mathfrak h_q, \mathfrak h_p)$, the three-state relation of Lemma 8.3, and $\omega(\lvert D\rvert) \le 5$ in Proposition 3.8.
