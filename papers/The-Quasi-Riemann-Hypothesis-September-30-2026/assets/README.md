## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the papers:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- The formula in the first panel reads $\sum n^{-\varepsilon} = \prod(1-p^{-\varepsilon})^{-1}$. It should be $\sum n^{-s} = \prod(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$.
- Typos: "Noureside" should be "Nonresidue", and "Guerenteed" should be "Guaranteed".
- The "critical strip" panel scatters zeros off the critical line. That's only a schematic; no zero off the line has ever been found.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- The first deck NotebookLM produced had three wrong slides. Slides 9 and 13 shaded the region *between 0 and 7/8* as "zero-free", when the zero-free region is the one *to the right of* 7/8. Slide 11 swapped the roles of the two computations: in the paper, theta-function reflection plus large-sieve bounds show the sum is *small*, and Poisson summation exposes the *large* signal a zero would create. All three were regenerated with `nlm slides revise`, and the shipped deck is the revised one. (A first revision attempt that also touched slide 10 garbled that slide, so it was discarded.)
- Slide 5 says Riemann "proved" the explicit formula. He stated it in 1859, and von Mangoldt gave a complete proof in 1895.
- Slide 10, step 1 draws the "ghost zero" on the 7/8 line and hatches the left side. The forbidden zone is the region to the *right* of 7/8.
- Slide 13 has a small typo: "Re(s) > = 1/2" should be "Re(s) = 1/2".

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 5, step 5 describes the "zero detector" as a positivity argument that forces a negative value on a square norm. That is **not** what the paper does. Its detector flags rows whose twisted $L$-functions have zeros, and those rows are then counted with moment estimates and the sextic large sieve. See [section 5 of the explainer](../README.md#5-the-main-idea-of-the-proof).
- Section 5, step 2 ("a foundational analytic/spectral signal shared across algebraic extensions") is vague and not how the paper puts it. The paper compares a character sum with a Mellin integral of $1/L$.
- Section 4 says the nonresidue bound comes from "Burgess-type bounds". It actually comes from the weak-GRH framework of Bhargava–Ivanyos–Mittal–Saxena.
- The Landau–Siegel bullet conflates two results. The 7/8 theorem already excludes every real zero in $(7/8,1)$. The $1-\beta \ge c/\log q$ statement is the separate companion result.
- The history table's rows for "August 2026 · Anthropic (Claude AI)" and "September 2026 · Youness Lamzouri" come from the Wikipedia source. They are **unverified** for this explainer and are unrelated to the paper.

**`study-guide.md`**
- Mostly built from Wikipedia. It covers RH background rather than this paper's proof.
- It repeats the unverified 2026 "Claude (Anthropic)" and "Lamzouri" critical-line claims (timeline, questions 8, glossary). Treat them as unverified.
- Its glossary entry says the quasi-RH puts "all non-trivial zeros … in a fixed zero-free half-plane". It should say there are *no* zeros in a fixed half-plane $\text{Re}(s) > \theta$ with $\theta < 1$.

**`mindmap-riemann-hypothesis.json`**
- It contains the same unverified 2026 critical-line nodes.
- It lists "Platt & Trudgian (2021): verified up to $t = 3\cdot10^{12}$" under zero-free regions. That is a numerical check of RH up to that height, not a zero-free region.
