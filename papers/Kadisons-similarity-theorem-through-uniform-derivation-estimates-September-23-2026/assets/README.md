## Errata

NotebookLM gets the big picture right but sometimes invents or garbles details. These are the known issues, found by reading each output against the paper. A common thread across files is the verification status. openai/math has a Lean scope document listing Comparator statements for this paper, but `lean/formalization.yaml` does not list the paper, its catalogue-wide `review` field reads `unchecked`, and nobody re-ran the build for this explainer. Wording such as "fully machine-verified" or "verified" therefore goes beyond what can be checked here.

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- The "Algebra" box in the first panel contains meaningless formulas (sums with "$O_l$", "$\rho(l)$", "$\perp.s = x$").
- In the question under it, "$S \in B(N)$" should be $S \in \mathcal{B}(H)$, "$\rho(a^s) = \rho(a)^s$" should be $\rho(a^{\ast}) = \rho(a)^{\ast}$, "C°-algebra" should be C\*-algebra, and "Adjoint Operation (+)" should read (\*).
- In the 2×2 panel, both axes of the left diagram are labelled "Range". Only the horizontal axis is the range; the kernel is the slanted line.
- The constant is printed as "$C = 3C_{\mathrm{lac}} + 2 \approx 1{,}192{,}231$". It is $C = 3C_{\mathrm{fac}} + 2 \approx 1{,}190{,}648$ (the large "≈ 1.19 million" beside it is right).
- The Kirchberg box writes the inner-derivation condition as "$\lambda(a) = [V, \sigma(a)]$". It should be $\Delta(a) = V\sigma(a) - \sigma(a)V$, for every faithful \*-representation $\sigma$.
- "$L = D + A_8$" should be the free product $L = D \ast A_0$.
- The "Structural case division" box says that type I and properly infinite factors are handled with "vector reductions and row/column derivation bounds". In the paper, finite type I factors are handled by averaging over the compact unitary group. Only the properly infinite case uses isometries together with the row and column bounds.
- The Lean panel says the files are "verifying main assertions" (see the note on verification status above).

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- The title, "A 83-Year Mathematical Journey", counts from Gelfand and Naimark's 1943 work. Kadison posed the question in 1955, 71 years before the preprint.
- The prompt's group-theory entries (Dixmier, 1950) are missing.
- The "1981–1986" panel groups Paulsen's 1984 theorem with the special cases. It is a general criterion (completely bounded homomorphisms are similar to complete contractions), not a special case.
- "Q.E.D." and "Finally Solved!" overstate the status of an unrefereed preprint.
- The formulas scribbled in the margins are decoration with no mathematical meaning.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- Slide 1 shows a single sheared operator $T$ becoming unitary as $S^{-1}TS$, with labels "skewed by $C > 0$" and "$\lVert\cdot\rVert_C$". None of this comes from the paper. The theorem is about homomorphisms of whole algebras, not single operators.
- Slide 4 puts a cross and "$\pi(x^{\ast}) \neq \pi(x)^{\ast}$" in the bounded-homomorphism column. Bounded homomorphisms are not *required* to respect adjoints, but some do: every \*-homomorphism is one.
- Slide 7 prints a sentence in quotation marks as if it were Kadison's own words. It is a paraphrase written by NotebookLM, not a quotation from his 1955 paper, and the portrait is a generic drawing, not a likeness.
- Slide 9 says the paper "officially solves" the conjecture; it is an unrefereed preprint. Its statement of the theorem also drops "unital".
- Slide 10: the curve in the plot is an illustration, not computed data, and its axis jumps from 2 to 4.
- Slide 11 merges two steps: averaging over the matrix unitary groups (Lemma 3.3) and the Schur–Weyl analysis of the averaged operator (Lemma 3.4). The labels on the bottom row ("bounded element", "rigid/orthogonal") do not correspond to anything in the paper.
- Slide 12 draws the argument as a closed loop, but the logic is a chain: estimate → completely bounded → inner → Kirchberg's theorem → similarity. "The case is definitively closed" overstates the preprint status.
- Slide 13's first bullet, "The distance from any operator to the algebra is universally bounded", is wrong. The corollary bounds $\mathrm{dist}(T, M)$ by $2C$ times the off-diagonal seminorm $\alpha_M(T)$, not by a universal number. The formula in the box is correct.
- Slide 14 overstates the verification status ("Verified" check marks, "Formally Verified in Lean (2026)", "checked, and forever inscribed in code"). Its subtitle, "The era of 'mostly sure' human proofs is over", is an editorial claim found in no source. The code shown in the editor panel is invented and is not Lean.
- Slides 2, 3, 5, 6 and 8 have no errors that we found. Slide 3 writes the C\*-identity as $\lVert xx^{\ast}\rVert = \lVert x\rVert^2$, which is equivalent to the usual $\lVert x^{\ast}x\rVert = \lVert x\rVert^2$.
- No revision was made: the shared generation quota had about 11% left, below the threshold for a revision. A single revision of slides 7, 13 and 14 would be the one to try.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 1 says the main results "have been fully machine-verified in the Lean 4 theorem prover" (see the note on verification status above).
- In the history table, the row "1986 — Christensen" also credits him with the derivation criterion (a derivation into $\mathcal{B}(H)$ is inner exactly when it is completely bounded). That is Christensen's 1977 result; the 1986 paper is the property Γ case.
- Section 5, Stage 2.1 says the corner $p\mathcal{C}p$ is isomorphic to $L^\infty(\mathbb{T}, m)$. It is the algebra of *even* functions in $L^\infty(\mathbb{T}, m)$, as the formula in the same sentence says.
- Section 5, Stage 2.4 says $G$ is approximated by right multipliers "on small corners $e\mathcal{L}e$ of trace $1/N$". Lemma 4.1 approximates $G$ on the subspaces $L^2(\mathcal{L})r$ for projections $r$ of trace at most $1/N$ (small right supports).
- The constants table has the right formulas but wrong values: $c_0 \approx 558.21$ should be $558.25$, $c_1 \approx 132{,}279.7$ should be $132{,}293.3$, $C_{\mathrm{fac}} \approx 396{,}841.1$ should be $396{,}882.0$, and $C \approx 1{,}190{,}525.3$ should be $1{,}190{,}647.9$.
- The glossary defines the similarity degree using "any completely bounded algebra homomorphism". It should say any bounded unital homomorphism; for completely bounded ones the inequality is not the point.
- The 2×2 example in Section 2 checks out, including the identity $D\pi(x^{\ast}) = \pi(x)^{\ast}D$ with $`D = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 1\\ 1 & 3\end{pmatrix}`$.

**`mindmaps.md` / `mindmap-proof.json`**
- "Nuclear case solved by Bunce, Christensen, Haagerup": the paper credits the nuclear case to Bunce and Christensen. Haagerup's 1983 contributions were the cyclic case, the complete-boundedness criterion, algebras without tracial states, and the amenability of nuclear algebras.
- "Central decomposition to remove separability": the central decomposition reduces von Neumann algebras on separable spaces to factors (Proposition 7.2). Separability is removed afterwards, by passing to separable reducing subspaces in the proof of Theorem 1.2.
- "Formalized in Lean by OpenAI (2026)" (see the note on verification status above).
