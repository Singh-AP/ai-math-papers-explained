### Notebook sources

The NotebookLM assets were generated in a notebook on a second NotebookLM account, with these sources:

1. `paper.pdf`: *An Upper Bound of 9/4 for the Matrix Multiplication Exponent* (openai/math, 2 Oct 2026)
2. *Complex Matrix Multiplication Below 2.258 and Rectangular Bounds* (openai/math, 24 Sep 2026), the companion
3. [`lean/docs/107.md`](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/107.md): scope of the Lean formalization
4. [Wikipedia: Computational complexity of matrix multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_complexity_of_matrix_multiplication), for historical background

The slides, both infographics and the audio used sources 1, 3 and 4. The report and the mind map used only sources 1 and 3. The 2.258 companion was in the notebook but was not selected for any generated asset, because its proof mechanism (Coppersmith–Winograd tensors, count windows, a numerical certificate) is different from this paper's.

## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the paper:

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- The first deck NotebookLM produced had six clearly wrong slides, and all six were regenerated with one `nlm slides revise`:
  - Slide 3 drew six subproblems for Strassen's recursion and called the 7 block products "scalar products".
  - Slide 5 said Coppersmith and Winograd introduced the laser method in 1990 (it is Strassen's, 1986–87), and its record labels were garbled.
  - Slide 6 had reversed and duplicated axis labels.
  - Slide 10 drew the ceiling as a decreasing curve.
  - Slide 12 invented a Lean theorem name and claimed "absolute mathematical certainty" that "does not rely on peer review".
  - Slide 13 confused the constant C with the complex numbers ℂ and asserted an "astronomically large" crossover that the paper never states.

  A diff with `scripts/diff_slides.py` confirmed that no other slide changed. What still remains after the revision:
- Slide 3 still draws only six `T(n/2)` boxes, although its text now correctly says 7 block multiplications.
- Slide 5's plateau labels still repeat entries ("Stothers 2010" twice, one reading "Stothers 2010 2012", "Le Gall 2014" twice, "two records in 2024" twice), and the label pointers don't sit at the right years. The values shown are correct. The staircase also skips Pan (1978), Bini et al. (1979), Coppersmith–Winograd (1981) and Strassen (1986).
- Slide 6 draws the grey 2.371177 line near 2.38.
- Slide 10's labels have typos: "(2a-1)(1/t)" should be (2a−1)^(1/t), and "a^(4/3 floor" is missing a parenthesis. The curve shapes are schematic.
- Slide 2 writes "at least O(n²)" where Ω(n²) is meant.
- Slide 4 places the ω = 2 marker slightly to the right of 2.0. This is schematic.
- Slide 7 says the 9/4 proof "introduces a new framework" of tensor characters. The framework is Strassen's asymptotic spectrum (1986–88). The paper's contribution is to combine explicit degenerations with this existing spectral viewpoint. The right-hand picture ("2.37", "α", "β", "γ" on shelves) is decorative and means nothing.
- Slide 8 says the proof "needs to find the maximum value of t". It actually shows t ≤ 3/4 for every character.
- Slide 9 places the (g−h)² weights inside the roots-of-unity filter box. In the paper they are a separate degeneration step after the filter.
- Slides 13 and 14 write O(n^2.25) without the +ε. Slide 13's third bullet gives the correct O(n^(9/4+ε)).
- Slide 15 says "we have shattered the 2.37 plateau", in OpenAI's voice, and calls ω = 2 "strictly quadratic time". ω = 2 would mean n^(2+o(1)).

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- The Strassen panel writes O(n^(log_x 7)); it should be log₂ 7. It also says "7 scalar multiplications" for 2 × 2 block products; these are 7 block multiplications.
- On the two exponent scales the pins are misplaced: "Naive O(n³)" sits to the right of 3, and Strassen's 2.807 pin sits near 2.9.
- The squeeze panel writes "ω = 3t ≤ 3 × (3/4)". For one character the matrix-multiplication exponent is 3t; ω itself is bounded through the detecting-character lemma.
- The "New Milestone" panel draws a smooth curve from 2.371177 to 2.25. This is schematic: the bound dropped in two preprints, not continuously.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- It calls the 2022–2026 stretch "Asymmetric Loss Fine-Tuning to the 2.25 Breakthrough". The asymmetric-hashing line of work led to 2.371177. The 9/4 proof uses a different method (characters and polynomial multiplication), not asymmetric loss analysis.
- It leaves out most dated records the prompt listed (Pan, Bini et al., Schönhage, Coppersmith–Winograd 1981, Strassen 1986, and the 24 September 2026 preprints).
- The 2 × 2 matrix sketch and the formula under "Duan et al. & Dupont et al." are garbled.
- "THE HOLY GRAIL: Conjectured Lower Bound ω = 2" mixes two facts: ω ≥ 2 is proved, and ω = 2 is the conjecture. The banner at the bottom right states this correctly.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- The "Developer Perspective" box and the glossary say tensor rank is the number of scalar multiplications in any division-free straight-line program, and that reducing it cuts "inner loop" instructions. Rank counts the multiplications of a *bilinear* algorithm. General programs can be cheaper, and rank is not an instruction count.
- History table:
  - The 2012–2013 row merges Davie–Stothers with Vassilevska Williams and gives 2.37286. Vassilevska Williams' 2012 bound was 2.3729.
  - The 2024–2025 row gives 2.37188, which is Duan–Wu–Zhou's 2022 bound. The 2024 records were 2.371552 and 2.371339.
  - The "2021–2024" Alman–Vassilevska Williams row credits them with "recursive combination barriers"; combination loss is Duan–Wu–Zhou's contribution.
  - Le Gall's and Alman–Vassilevska Williams' bounds are truncated to 2.372863 and 2.372859, slightly below the proved values 2.3728639 and 2.3728596.
- Section 1 rounds log₂ 7 to 2.8073; it is 2.80735…, usually written 2.8074.
- Section 4 says the method "bypasses the structural combination-loss barriers inherent to the laser method". The paper makes no such claim.
- Section 6 calls the fixed-point theorem's author "Alfred Tychonoff". He was Andrey (Andrei) Tychonoff.
- Section 7 calls the staggered-extraction companion "the companion formalization paper". It is a companion preprint whose bound is listed as formalized.
- Section 5 (the proof walk-through) matches the paper, including Proposition 3.1's substitutions and weights, Lemma 5.1's recursion and the final recursion step.

**`mindmaps.md` / `mindmap-proof.json`**
- Under "Upper Bound Theorem" it lists ω(F) < 2.371054886006746 for all fields, the dual exponent α > 0.465 and the rectangular bound next to ω ≤ 9/4. These come from the two companions, via the Lean scope document, not from this paper.
- "Coppersmith–Winograd Method → Convex optimization & asymmetric refinements" lumps Le Gall's convex optimization and the 2022–2026 asymmetric work under Coppersmith and Winograd.
