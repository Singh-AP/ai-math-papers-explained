## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the papers:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- Panel 1 labels the Max-Cut constant "α<sub>OW</sub> ≈ 0.878". It should be α<sub>GW</sub>. "Goemans–Williamson (1995)" is the journal year; the algorithm appeared in 1994.
- Panel 2's left diagram ("Rule: Label(A) = Label(B) + 1 mod 3", with garbled arrows) is not a readable permutation rule. The XOR triangle on the right is correct.
- Panel 3 writes the YES case as "value ≥ 1 − c". It should be 1 − ε. The drawn person is a decorative illustration, not a portrait.
- Panel 4 says "OpenAI Researchers". The paper is credited to "OpenAI", and openai/math says its results were produced by an internal OpenAI model. "Strict Gap Guarantees over Finito Alphabet F₃^φ" should read "a fixed finite alphabet F₂ˢ".
- Panel 5 says a high-rank linear reading detects the noise "at least 1/6 of the time". The paper's bound is **1/8** (Lemma 3.1).
- Panel 6 truncates α<sub>GW</sub> to 0.8785 (it is 0.87857…).
- Panel 7 says the "main theorem and consequences [are] formally verified in Lean 4". openai/math lists the main theorem (and the companions' Max-Cut and Vertex Cover gap theorems) as formalized. The Section 8 consequences are not formalized, and this explainer did not re-run the check.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- "Majority Is Stablest Theorem (Mussel et al., 2005)" should be Mossel, O'Donnell and Oleszkiewicz.
- The formulas scattered around the drawing (for example "∧∀(λc⇒∞) = {λ" and "Dλ = Eα + Sλ") are meaningless decoration.
- The "HARDNESS RESOLVED" badge overstates the result. These are NP-hardness statements; P vs NP is untouched.
- The 2002 Dinur–Safra bound for Vertex Cover (1.36) is missing, though the prompt listed it.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`** (not revised; see below for the revision that would fix the worst slides)
- Slide 3 shows a stray colour name, "Vermilion", twice. Its Vertex Cover axis runs from 2.0 down to 1.0 but draws √2 ≈ 1.414 on the wrong side of the 1.75 tick. Neither axis is to scale.
- Slide 7 suggests that each 3SAT clause is mapped to one parity equation. Håstad's reduction is a PCP-based construction, not a clause-by-clause translation.
- Slide 8's footer says the gadget "perfectly isolates the signal from the noise". Honest answers still fail up to $p/2$ of the time. "Non-Linear Matrix Filter" is the slide's own name for what the paper calls the latent alphabet gadget.
- **Slide 9 is wrong.** It is titled "Extracting Structure (The YES Case)" and draws a "Decoder C Boundary". In the paper this step is the soundness analysis. It starts from an *arbitrary* labeling that passes 99% of tests and is used to rule out the NO case. The decoder C belongs only to the honest labeling. The three bullet points themselves are correct.
- **Slide 11 is wrong.** It places Vertex Cover inside Raghavendra's theorem for constraint satisfaction problems. The paper derives the Vertex Cover threshold from Khot–Regev's separate reduction ("The Vertex Cover result uses its own reduction"). The footer text is cut off at the right edge, and "permanently defined" overstates.
- Slide 12 says the two algorithms are "proven absolutely optimal". They are optimal among polynomial-time algorithms *if P ≠ NP*. The struck-through 16/17 appears twice.
- **Slide 14 is wrong.** Its formulas and "Lean code" (a rank-one test with $v^{\top}Mv\ge\alpha$, power iteration, `Eigenvalues.PowerIteration`, `sum_le_C`) are invented and have nothing to do with the paper or with openai/math's Lean files. The real formal statement is `theorem11 (ε δ : ℝ) … : Nonempty (Explicit.MachineOutputContract.BinaryGapReduction ε δ)`. The "100% Verified in Lean" badge also overstates. openai/math lists the theorem as formalized, the catalogue's review status is "unchecked", and this explainer did not re-run the check.
- Slide 15 says P vs NP stays open "because the result relies on reductions". More precisely, NP-hardness says that a fast algorithm for these thresholds would give one for 3SAT; it does not say whether 3SAT itself is easy.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 1 says the order of quantifiers "is the fundamental reason the Unique Games Conjecture remained open" and that the conjecture "rules out all such alphabet-dependent rounding algorithms". Both claims are wrong. The paper says that known algorithms (Charikar–Makarychev–Makarychev, Arora–Barak–Steurer) do not *contradict* the conjecture, because their guarantees degrade as the alphabet grows or need subexponential time.
- Section 2 groups events into loose ranges that mostly use journal years. FGLSS (1991) is placed in "1992–1998", Håstad (1997) in "2001–2002", Dinur–Safra (2002), Khot–Regev (2003) and KKMO (2004) in "2005–2008", and Mossel–O'Donnell–Oleszkiewicz (2005) in "2010–2012". The explainer uses conference years instead. Håstad's 16/17 for Max-Cut also needs the Trevisan–Sorkin–Sudan–Williamson gadgets.
- Section 4 says the results become "unconditional mathematical theorems". They are NP-hardness theorems, which limit algorithms only if P ≠ NP. It also claims "exact, tight NP-hardness thresholds for … Multicut, Sparsest Cut, Correlation Clustering". For those problems the paper proves that *no* constant factor is achievable (Corollary 8.3), which is not a tight threshold. It says the Vertex Cover companion starts "from 2-to-1 / Label Cover sources", but that companion starts from ordinary Label Cover only. "Continuous cube resampling" is not how the Max-Cut companion describes its row resampling (**unverified** wording).
- Section 5, step 1 calls the NO case "almost entirely unsatisfiable (1/2 + ε)". At most about half of the equations can be satisfied, so about half fail.
- Section 5, step 3 merges two things: the latent alphabet gadget (Section 3 of the paper: the space $\mathcal V$, decoder $C$ and noise $\mu$) and the table keys and folding (Section 4.3).
- Section 5, step 4 says the test is there "to enforce that candidate label transformations act as pure linear translations". The test checks equality of restored answers; the translation form of the constraints comes from folding.
- Section 5, step 6 ("Detects linear readings of high matrix rank with probability at least 1/8") has it backwards. It is the latent *noise* that is detected, with probability at least 1/8, by every family of linear functionals whose restriction to $K$ has rank at least $r_{\ast}$ (Lemma 3.1).
- Section 5, step 8 calls $\beta=k^{-2/3}$ a "noise parameter". It is the probability that a block is projected to a single variable.
- The people table credits Dinur and Steurer with "clean coordinate techniques". The clean-coordinate argument is the paper's own (following Khot–Minzer–Safra's vanishing-advice framework); Dinur–Steurer supply the repetition bound. The table also says Khot–Minzer–Safra "developed the inverse shortcode framework". The paper derives its inverse shortcode theorem (Theorem 5.1) from their Grassmann expansion theorem through Barak–Kothari–Steurer's matrix chart.
- Section 7, item 1 says the theorem "establishes NP-hardness statements under the standard assumption that P ≠ NP". This is wrong: NP-hardness is proved outright, and P ≠ NP is needed only to conclude that no polynomial-time algorithm exists. Item 4 calls the Lean formalization an "ongoing technical process". openai/math lists the main theorem as formalized with a Comparator statement; the catalogue's review status reads "unchecked".
