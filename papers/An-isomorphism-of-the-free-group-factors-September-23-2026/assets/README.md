### The slide revision

The first deck had three clearly wrong slides:

- Slide 10 showed an invented word structure ($`w = g_1 g_2 \cdots`$, $`p_m = g_1 \cdots g_m`$, $`w = p_m g_m h`$) instead of the paper's $`p_j = (ab_1)\cdots(ab_j)`$ and $`w_m = p_m a`$. Its last sentence came from an abandoned idea in the reasoning summary.
- Slide 11 drew a bump supported away from 0, labelled "completely empty gap at origin". The paper's limit law has density $`\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{(4-x)/x}`$, which is unbounded at 0. The point is that it has no *atom* at 0, not that there is a gap.
- Slide 15 showed invented Lean 3 code (`interpolated_factors_isomorphism`, `flow_existence`, `begin … end`) and meaningless formulas, including $`\delta_r(\mathcal L(\mathcal F_r)) = r`$, which contradicts the paper's Corollary 7.1. It also said the result was "entirely formalized and verified".

One revision (deck `701d6b3f`, from `fdd2e9f5`) regenerated exactly these three slides. Slides 11 and 15 are now correct, and slide 10 is much better; its remaining flaw is listed below. The shipped deck is the revised one.

## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the paper:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- The definition box reads $`L(G) = \lbrace\lambda(g) : g \in G\rbrace^{n}`$. It should be the double commutant $`\lbrace\lambda(g)\rbrace''`$. The trace diagram labels the vector $`\delta_c`$ instead of $`\delta_e`$.
- In the "Amplification Bridge" panel, the middle formula reads $`L(F_3)^{\sqrt2} \cong L(F_3)`$, and "Combining" reads $`L(F_3) \cong L(F_3)`$. They should be $`L(\mathbb{F}_5)^{\sqrt2} \cong L(\mathbb{F}_3)`$ and $`L(\mathbb{F}_3) \cong L(\mathbb{F}_5)`$. The first formula, $`L(\mathbb{F}_3)^{\sqrt2} \cong L(\mathbb{F}_2)`$, is right.
- The corner panel's labels are garbled ("Normalized normalized corners", "Normalized all corners").
- The mechanism panel's sentence "making $`C`$, the word they imitate $`C`$" is garbled. In the paper the flow first makes a word close to $`C A_w`$, and then *undoing* the basis change $`C \mapsto C A_w`$ ($`\alpha = \gamma^{-1}\circ\beta_1`$) makes it close to $`C`$. The error budgets are $`\varepsilon_k, r_k`$, not "$`\tau_k`$".
- "Formally verified in Lean" overstates what this explainer could check. The Lean scope document says the formalization proves the result, but the paper is not listed in `lean/formalization.yaml`, and nobody here built the proof.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- The intro says the question was open "for nearly a century" and was "finally solved". The free group factors date from 1943, and the solution is an unrefereed preprint.
- The banner "(1929–1994)" sits over "Murray and von Neumann Establish Factor Theory". It is the span of the whole left half, not the dates of their work (1936–1943).
- The formulas under "1985–1991" and around the 2026 brain are decorative nonsense (for example $`\Sigma + z/n = \dots(\mathbb{F})^n`$). The parameter line drawn for interpolated factors runs from 0 to 1, while the parameter is $`r > 1`$.
- "1992–1994" for interpolated factors merges Rădulescu's 1992 fundamental-group theorem with the 1994 interpolation papers.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`** (revised deck)
- Slide 1 says "Solved" and "an 80-year-old mystery". Read "solved" as "claimed in an unrefereed preprint".
- Slide 2 draws the same small tree for $`\mathbb{F}_2`$ and $`\mathbb{F}_3`$. These are not their Cayley graphs, which are 4-regular and 6-regular trees.
- Slide 3 calls $`L(G)`$ "the infinite-dimensional limit of matrix mechanics". That is misleading for free groups: the free group factors are exactly the ones that are *not* limits of matrix algebras (they are not hyperfinite).
- Slide 5's title is garbled ("The linelling of 90-year search"), and the faint formulas in the background are decorative.
- Slide 6, "The Graveyard of Invariants", presents the *model's own* abandoned attempts from the reasoning summary as if they were the history of the field. Its "Failed: …" reasons are paraphrases of that summary, not results in the paper. The sentence "Every known invariant fails" is true only as a consequence of the theorem.
- Slide 8 draws a shrinking square for an amplification by $`t = \sqrt2 > 1`$, which enlarges. The formulas on the slide are correct.
- Slide 9 labels two boxes $`A_n`$, and its arrows sweep toward $`C`$, but the flow fixes $`C`$.
- Slide 10 (revised): the definitions on the top line are now right. The expanded word below repeats $`(ab_1)`$ and puts the braces under single blocks rather than the cumulative prefixes $`p_j = (ab_1)\cdots(ab_j)`$. The correct expansion is $`w_m = (ab_1)(ab_2)\cdots(ab_m)\,a`$. "Coeficient" is misspelled.
- Slide 12 lists "4. Perturb the tuple" after "3. Fix the future error budget". In the paper, the perturbation and its witness word come together in one step, and only then is the future budget $`r_k`$ chosen.
- Slide 13 credits the conditional expectation with proving $`L^2`$ density. In the paper, the error budgets give the $`L^2`$ density, and the conditional expectation then turns density into generation.
- Slide 14 repeats "integer integer".
- Slide 15 (revised) is accurate, but its title "Mechanized Truth" overstates what this explainer could verify (see the explainer's section 7).

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 1.3 attributes the conjugacy argument for factoriality to Murray and von Neumann; the paper gives the argument without that attribution. The phrase "conjugating $`g`$ by $`a^k g a^{-k}`$" is garbled. It should say that the conjugates $`a^k g a^{-k}`$ have growing length.
- Section 2.1 says Kadison "formally raised" the problem. The paper says only that Dykema's introduction credits Kadison with raising it.
- Sections 2.3 and Step 9 call the isomorphism "explicit". It is built as a limit of infinitely many choices; see the explainer's section 7.
- Step 5 says $`\nu(\lbrace0\rbrace) = 0`$ proves "$`Q_m/m`$ has zero atomic mass at the origin". Only the *limit* law has no atom. The paper explicitly allows $`Q_m`$ itself to have a kernel, which is why it uses a cutoff.
- Step 8 writes $`X^{w_k}`$ for the word $`X_{w_k}`$.
- Its formula $`\tau(Q_m^r) = \mathrm{Cat}_r m^r + O(m^{r-1})`$ is equivalent to the paper's $`\mathrm{Cat}_r (m)_r + O(m^{r-1})`$. The rest of the proof walk-through (Steps 1–9 and the Lean table) matches the paper and `lean/docs/287.md`.
