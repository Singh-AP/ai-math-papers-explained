### Notebook sources

The notebook was built on a second NotebookLM account and has three sources:

1. `paper.pdf`: *The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable* (openai/math, 23 Sep 2026)
2. [`lean/docs/158.md`](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/158.md): scope of the Lean formalization
3. [Wikipedia: Hadwiger–Nelson problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadwiger%E2%80%93Nelson_problem), for historical background

The slides, both infographics and the audio used all three sources. The report and the mind map used only sources 1 and 2.

## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the paper and the openai/math Lean docs:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- "What Was Known": the caption says de Grey's graph has "1,881" vertices. It has 1,581, as the panel above it correctly says.
- The Moser spindle icon is a 5-point pentagram, not the 7-point spindle. The three dates are drawn out of order (1961, 1950, 2018).
- "Proof Part 1" shows an invented formula, "$f_i = \frac{\varepsilon}{\pi} f \beta_i$", which appears nowhere in the paper.
- "Current Status": "finite 6-chromatic graph: not exhibited" should say a finite unit-distance graph that is *not 5-colorable*, whose chromatic number would be 6 or 7. "Formally verified in Lean 4" is how the repository lists the two statements; this explainer did not re-run the check, and the catalogue's review status is "unchecked".
- Typo: "unit-circie".

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- **"2025–2026: Sokolov–Voronov (2025) and OpenAI (2026) proved 5 colors fail" is wrong.** Sokolov and Voronov proved that *polygonal* colorings need at least 7 colors. Only the OpenAI paper shows that five colors fail for arbitrary colorings.
- "Isbell, Hadwiger, and the Mosers proved bounds of 4 to 7": Nelson himself obtained the lower bound 4 (1950). Isbell found the hexagonal 7-coloring, Hadwiger published the bounds 4 and 7 (1961), and the Mosers gave the 7-point spindle (1961).
- "De Grey and Polymath raise the bound to 5": de Grey raised the bound. The Polymath16 project and others then found *smaller* graphs (Heule 553 vertices, Parts 509).
- The pictured graphs and colorings are decorative; none of them is a correct unit-distance construction.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- **Slide 1:** the cover graph is decorative. It is not the Moser spindle or any unit-distance graph from the paper.
- **Slide 2:** "In 1950, Edward Nelson and Hugo Hadwiger asked …". Nelson posed the problem in 1950; Hadwiger published it in 1961 (and a related covering result in 1945).
- **Slide 3:** the hexagon picture is not a valid 7-coloring: the hexagons are irregular and overlapping, and there is a blank gap. The text's "In the 1950s" is vague; the paper dates the problem to 1950 and credits Isbell with the hexagonal coloring.
- **Slide 4:** the text is right, but the three panels are wrong. In step 1 two corners of the unit triangle are both blue, which is not a proper coloring. Step 2 is not the Moser spindle. Step 3 marks a red–yellow pair as the conflict; in the real argument the conflict is between the two tips, which are forced to have the *same* color. See the [hand-made figure](figures/moser-spindle.svg).
- **Slide 5:** the caption is right, but the number line is garbled. The "1" box appears twice, and an unlabeled crossed-out box sits between 4 and 5. The 1961 Moser arrow points at that box, and the 2026 arrow points at 7. The actual story: 1950–1961 gives at least 4, 2018 rules out 4 (at least 5), 2026 rules out 5 (at least 6), and 7 is the upper bound.
- **Slide 6:** "definitively 6 or 7" overstates the status: the result is a preprint, formalized in Lean but not yet peer reviewed.
- **Slide 7:** the second sentence is garbled text ("a coloring could threnit the exists on emuo wtmt of cooloing tha").
- **Slide 8:** the left panel equates "measurable" with neat region boundaries. Measurable color classes can still be very irregular. The paper's distinction is between arbitrary (possibly non-measurable) color classes and measurable ones; regular "map-type" colorings are a third, narrower setting.
- **Slide 9:** "a smooth, measurable 5-coloring" is not what the transfer gives. Theorem 1.3 produces a *weak measurable* coloring: measurable classes, proper almost everywhere, with no smoothness. The averaging is over the countable group of algebraic shifts and rotations, not "infinite shifts". The retained fields are continuous under translation in the $L^2$ sense, not pointwise continuous color fields.
- **Slide 10:** the figure has 12 spokes 30° apart but labels every gap "60°". The boxed reason for "at most 2 colors per palette" ("because 5 colors must be shared among these mutually exclusive directions") is not the paper's argument. The sixty-degree exclusion only makes palettes 60° apart disjoint. The bound of two (Proposition 5.6) comes from a separate lemma: centers whose palettes alternate between one fixed pair of colors are uniformly separated (Lemma 5.5).
- **Slide 11:** a typo ("moving, moving"), and a misleading mechanism. The loop of colors in the paper is not found by walking around the unit circle of palettes. It is a loop around a small disk near a special ("cyclic") center, in a continuous map from a square to the complete graph on the five colors, forced by a cover obstruction (Lemma 7.5).
- **Slide 12:** broadly right. "Immediately break the rules of geometry" and "a massive open region" overstate: the 4- and 5-cycle cases take several lemmas, and the three-color region is an open region near the common point $x$.
- **Slide 13:** the drawn graph is not the Moser spindle. "We proved in 1961" should be: Leo and William Moser found the spindle in 1961.
- **Slide 14:** largely invented.
  - The figure labeled "Moser Spindle, 7 Points, 11 Edges" is a 5-point pentagram.
  - The Lean code is fictional and partly garbled. There is no `moser_spindle_chromatic_number` theorem among the Comparator statements; the formalized statements are `OAI.EuclideanFiveColor.no_proper_five_coloring` and `OAI.Problem160.properColoring_seven` (see [`lean/docs/158.md`](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/158.md)).
  - "Absolute certainty" and "completely formalized … doesn't just rely on human peer review" overclaim. Two statements are formalized, the catalogue's project-wide review status is "unchecked", and the paper has not been peer reviewed.
- **Slide 15:** no errors found.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 4 is headed "Resolving a 76-Year-Old Open Question". The question is **not** resolved: the paper rules out 5 and leaves 6 versus 7 open, as the report itself says in Section 7.
- Section 7, item 3 misstates the Lean scope. It calls the formalization a "computer-verified grounding for the classical foundation" of a "baseline" bound. In fact `lean/docs/158.md` lists the paper's *new* theorem (no proper five-coloring, for arbitrary colorings) and the seven-coloring as the formalized results. It also calls the paper a "theoretical computer science" preprint; it is a mathematics preprint.
- The Section 2 table and Section 6 call Townsend "A.B. Townsend". His initials are S. P.
- Step 4 says the thresholded vectors map into "the 1-skeleton of the 5-simplex". Probability vectors on five labels form the **4-simplex**, whose 1-skeleton is the complete graph $K_5$.
- The 3-cycle step says "in polar coordinates $z = \xi + i\upsilon$". These are Cartesian coordinates.
- Section 2 says that Nelson proved the lower bound 4 "by constructing small finite unit-distance graphs", and that Exoo–Ismailescu's proof is "computer-assisted". Neither detail is in the paper; treat both as **unverified**. The report also attributes the $r = 2/5$ version of the hexagon coloring to Isbell; the paper gives it as the construction "described by Hadwiger (1961)". And Hadwiger's 1961 publication was a problem column (*Ungelöste Probleme* Nr. 40), not a "formal journal paper".
- Corollary 1.5 omits that the invariant measure $\mu$ on $E(2)$ is *finitely additive* and defined on all subsets. That distinction matters: no countably additive invariant probability exists on the plane's isometry group.
- The proof-pipeline diagram labels the averaging output "Invariant Probability Measure ν on Dual Group D". The averaging gives an invariant law on proper labelings; measures on $D$ appear only as spectral measures.
- Section 4 says the transfer shows that non-measurable colorings "contain no secret structural advantages over measurable ones". Theorem 1.3 is an equivalence with *weak* measurable colorings, which may have a null set of bad pairs. It is not an equivalence with measurable proper colorings.
- The ASCII picture of the placed Moser spindle in the 3-cycle step has wrong edges. It is schematic only; the paper's Figure 3 shows the actual placement.
- Section 7 speaks of a "finite 6-chromatic unit-distance graph". The paper gives only a finite graph that is not 5-colorable; its chromatic number would be 6 or 7.

**`mindmap-proof.json` / `mindmaps.md`**
- "Sokolov & Voronov: polygonal lower bound 7" is filed under "Measurable Colorings". Their result concerns polygonal (map-type) colorings, not measurable ones.
- "7-Point Rational Certificate (Moser Graph Placement)" is filed under "Formalization & Verification". The certificate is part of the paper's own written proof (Lemma 8.9), not of the Lean formalization.
- "Falconer & Payne: $\chi_m(\mathbb{R}^2) \ge 5$": Falconer proved this bound (1981). Payne developed the density-point method further (2009).
