## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the papers:

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- This is the **revised** deck. One `nlm slides revise` regenerated slides 2, 3, 7, 8 and 11; `scripts/diff_slides.py` confirms that the other ten slides are unchanged from the first deck. The revision fixed slides 7, 8 and 11. It did not fix the curve drawings on slides 2 and 3, and it added new label errors on slide 2.
- Slides 2 and 3 still draw the curve $y^2 + y = x^3 - x$ wrongly. The real curve has two separate pieces: an oval through $(0,0)$, $(-1,0)$, $(-1,-1)$, $(0,-1)$, and an unbounded branch through $(1,0)$, $(1,-1)$, $(2,2)$ and $(2,-3)$. It is symmetric about the line $y = -1/2$. Both slides draw one connected curve that is symmetric about the $x$-axis. See the [hand-made curve figure](figures/curve-37a1-rational-points.png) for the correct picture.
- Slide 2 marks $(-1, 1)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$, which are not on the curve, and labels two of them "(−1.11)" and "(0.84)". Those two numbers are the $x$-coordinates where the oval and the unbounded branch begin, attached to the wrong points. The slide also puts the label "(−1, 0)" on a point drawn at about $(-1.4, 0)$, and draws $(2,-3)$ at about $(2,-2.6)$. Its labels for $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(-1,-1)$, $(0,-1)$, $(1,-1)$ and $(2,2)$ are correct.
- Slide 3's labels ($P = (0,0)$, $3P = (-1,-1)$, the line $y = x$, the third point $(2,2)$ and $4P = (2,-3)$) are correct, but the points are plotted at approximate positions, with $4P$ near $(1.85, -2)$. The revision added a dot labelled $(1,0)$ that is drawn off the curve, and an unlabelled dashed horizontal line near $y = -0.3$. The curve's mirror line is $y = -1/2$.
- Slide 4 is a schematic. The multiples of one point form a single chain $P, 2P, 3P, \ldots$, not the branching tree it draws.
- The curve sketch on slide 6 is decorative. It is not a drawing of any particular curve.
- Slide 7 was **fixed** by the revision. It now defines the real period as the integral of the Néron differential over $E(\mathbb{R})$, the regulator as the determinant of the canonical height pairing, the Tamagawa numbers as $`c_p = [E(\mathbb{Q}_p) : E_0(\mathbb{Q}_p)]`$, and torsion as the number of rational points of finite order. (The first deck called $\Omega$ an "area" and garbled the other three.) The revision also gave this slide a dark-blue background that differs from the rest of the deck.
- Slide 8 was **fixed** by the revision. It now gives the real period $\Omega = 5.98691729$ and the regulator $0.05111141$ for 37a1, with torsion, Tamagawa number and Sha all 1. It also says the conjecture dates from the early 1960s and has been a Millennium Prize Problem since 2000. (The first deck mislabelled the two numbers and said BSD had been a Millennium Prize Problem "for over 60 years".)
- Slide 9 is titled "A Century-Long Bridge", but the conjecture is about 60 years old. It also has the typo "the the".
- Slide 10 says that on 3 October 2026 the model "produced a catalog of preprints". Only the principal paper is dated 3 October. The two companions are dated 24 September 2026.
- Slide 11 was **fixed** by the revision. It now says the principal paper proves $X_p(E) \ge 0$ for every curve without complex multiplication of analytic rank 0 or 1 (Section 5, Proposition 5.11), using Kato's zeta elements. (The first deck said mathematicians "already knew" this.)
- Slide 12 has the typo "≥= 0".
- Slide 13 calls family 006 "a framework". It is a separate openai/math result family (Goldfeld's density conjecture, 23 Sep 2026). Its twist-density theorem combines with this paper to give full BSD for a density-one set of quadratic twists. The scatter plot is decorative.
- Slide 14 lists analytic rank 0 and 1 over $\mathbb{Q}$ as "Solved". That is the papers' claim, in unrefereed preprints. The gloss "where Heegner points cannot directly reach" is not from the papers and is **unverified** here.
- Slide 15 says the work "bridges a century-old gap" (about 60 years) and calls the logic "groundbreaking and highly detailed". That is an evaluation, not something this explainer checked.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- The history box in section 2 misdates several results. Rubin's main-conjecture paper is from 1991, not the "1970s–1980s". Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor is from 2001, not the "1980s–1990s". Burungale–Flach is from 2024 (arXiv 2022), not the "2000s–2010s".
- Section 3's table says the converse companion's method is "rationalized Galois cohomology, Euler systems". The companion's own summary describes tame deformations, theta series on a unitary group of signature (3,1), and the extraction of Galois extensions.
- Section 4 says the theorem itself shows that 100% of twists satisfy BSD. That density statement is the combination with family 006 (stated in the openai/math summary of family 002). The principal paper uses the twist-density theorem only to choose auxiliary fields. It also says the converse companion "solves the classical Diophantine problem $x^3 + y^3 = n$". It proves that every prime $\ell \equiv 4, 7, 8 \pmod 9$ is a sum of two rational cubes, and the companion notes that these cases were also treated by Yin and by Burungale–Tian.
- The flowchart in section 5 credits $U_w \in R$ to "Chai–Hida rigidity". Integrality comes from the high-character division test; Chai–Hida gives $p \nmid B_w$. The prose below the flowchart gets this right. Step 6 also skips the centre computation of Proposition 10.1: the unit gives equal valuations of the analytic and algebraic sides, and the lattice computation then turns this into $X_p(E) + X_p(E^D) = 0$.
- The people table has wrong initials: "W. Zagier" should be D. (Don) Zagier, and "C. Smith" should be A. (Alexander) Smith. It lists Skinner, Urban, Jetchev and Wan as providing "local division inputs". The paper says their theorems "are not used here as unrestricted odd-primary formulas"; they are context, not inputs. The description of what Serre and Bogomolov's results ensure ("non-vanishing of Galois evaluations on deep $`SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)`$ subgroups") is not from the paper and is **unverified** here.
- Section 7 calls the paper "mathematically rigorous in its structural logic". That is an evaluation, not something this explainer checked.
- The glossary describes the real period as a "physical perimeter or loop length". It is the integral of the Néron differential over $E(\mathbb{R})$.

**`mindmap-proof.json` / `mindmaps.md`**
- "Integral character division proves $U_w$ in R and $p \nmid U_w$": the absence of the factor $p$ comes from vertical primitivity ($p \nmid B_w$, Section 6), not from character division.
- "New tame directions force quotient codimension ≥ 2": in the paper it is the locus of *simultaneous jumps* that has codimension at least 2.
- "Proves full-index discrepancy identity: $X_p(E) + X_p(E^D) = 0$" mixes two statements. The full-index discrepancy identity is Proposition 2.8 (the sum equals a Heegner-index expression), and its vanishing is Corollary 10.2.
