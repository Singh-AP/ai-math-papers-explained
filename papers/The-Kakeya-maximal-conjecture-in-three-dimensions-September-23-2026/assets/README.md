## Errata

NotebookLM gets the big picture right and is good at visuals, but it garbles some details. Known issues, found by reading each output against the papers:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- Typos: "Besicoviteh's" (Besicovitch's), "arbitrarly" (arbitrarily), "Córduba" (Córdoba). The badges in panel 5 read "AI Otianed" and "AI Obhished".
- The panels are numbered 1, 2, 4, 3 in reading order: panels 3 and 4 are swapped.
- Panel 1's picture (a triangle and a curved needle path labelled "Measure Zero Set") is decorative, not a construction.
- Panel 3 marks two lengths "1" on the tube and never marks the radius $\delta$. "Integration Density Across Tube" is not a term from the papers.
- Panel 5's row for dimension 3 reads "Resolved Claim (2023–2026)". It merges the human-proved set theorem (Wang–Zahl, 2025) with the AI-claimed maximal theorem (September 2026).
- Panel 6's "4-Coordinate Information Matrix" and its "Spatial/Trajectory Axis" labels are decorative. The paper's four coordinates are positions $X, Y$ and velocities $U, V$.

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- It skips Davies (1971), Córdoba (1977), Pál (1920) and Bourgain's 1991 paper.
- The "2000" panel merges Bourgain's 1999 arithmetic method, Katz–Łaba–Tao (2000) and Katz–Tao (2002).
- The headline "September 2026: 4D dimension settled" overstates an unreviewed preprint. The caption under it ("OpenAI preprints claim …") is accurate.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- Slide 1: the background formulas are decorative and partly garbled: "Area(D) = π", "f = u𝔤v ⋯ 1", "L(x) ⊂ D". The formula $f^{\ast}(x) = \sup \lvert \frac{1}{\lvert B\rvert}\int_B f\rvert$ is the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function over balls, not the Kakeya maximal function.
- Slide 2: the deltoid's area $\pi/8 \approx 0.392$ is NotebookLM's own number and was not checked. The disk ($\pi/4$) and Pál's triangle ($1/\sqrt3$, 1920) are right.
- Slide 3: "In 1920, Abram Besicovitch proved there is no minimum area. A needle can be fully rotated…". The needle result is from 1928. The 1919–1920 result is the planar set of measure zero with a segment in every direction.
- Slide 6: "Union Volume ≥ λⁿ × Total Volume" leaves out the constant and the $\delta^{\varepsilon}$ loss.
- Slide 9: the bush picture is right, but its captions ("volume is maximized" at the edges, "the math hits a physical limit") are NotebookLM's own gloss. The actual computation is in [section 5](../README.md#a-worked-calculation-why-the-exponent-is-3) of the explainer.
- Slide 12: the title says "Solved"; "claimed" is accurate for an unreviewed preprint. The plotted profile bends smoothly upward after $\tau$, but the paper's canonical profile $F(s) = \beta(s-\tau)_+$ is flat and then exactly linear, with a corner at $\tau$.
- Slide 13: the title says "Solved". The regime-1 caption ("Tubes form a polynomial potential. The field is switched to become approximately conservative") garbles the paper. There, switching between lines makes an interpolated velocity field approximately conservative, which yields a potential. The regime-2 caption leaves out the other branch of that case (wide projected intervals). "Mathematically crush the tubes" (regime 3) is not the paper's description: full-time localization and a scalar projection drive the time cost to zero.
- Slide 14: "The Kakeya problem governs Fourier restriction" overstates the link. The restriction conjecture implies the Kakeya maximal conjecture, and not conversely. "Like light or sound" and "predict wave interference patterns" are NotebookLM's own gloss.
- Slide 15: "SOLVED (AI, 2026)" for both results; they are unreviewed preprints. "Dimensions five and higher remain entirely open" is true of the full conjectures, but the companion does give dimension at least 4 there.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- The title ("Resolving the Four-Dimensional Kakeya Conjecture") centres the companion rather than the principal three-dimensional paper.
- Section 1 swaps Besicovitch's results. It says he proved in 1919 that a needle can be turned in arbitrarily small area and "subsequently" found measure-zero sets. The measure-zero set came first (1919–1920) and the needle result in 1928. It also calls the deltoid "a symmetric convex figure"; a deltoid is not convex.
- Section 2 says "Hausdorff dimension requires no compactness, measurability, or structural regularity assumptions on K or its witnessing line family". That is a feature of the companion's theorem, not of the definition.
- Section 3 claims that the one-line maximal-to-volume argument gives both Minkowski and Hausdorff dimension $n$. It gives only Minkowski dimension. Hausdorff dimension needs the shaded, density-uniform form and dyadic pigeonholing (see section 1.6 of the explainer).
- Section 4 says Wang–Zahl's set proofs "relied on … sticky tube families". Their final theorem covers arbitrary Kakeya sets; stickiness was an earlier special case. The principal paper's point is the density dependence $\lambda^{K(\varepsilon)}$.
- Section 5 mixes the two papers. Step 1 uses the companion's notation ($x(t) = y_0 + tV$, bins of length $\rho = D^{-1}$). Steps 2 and 6 describe $h(p,z)$ as a "time-localization cost" and talk of "horizon parameters", which belong to the companion; the principal paper's $h$ is the least extra power of $N$ in its critical inequality. Step 3 names "determinant multilinear Kakeya" and "planebrush" estimates. The principal paper uses Bennett–Carbery–Tao and Guth's multilinear Kakeya, the planar Furstenberg estimate and the Guth–Wang–Zahl set estimate, and no planebrushes. Its note that the weighted plank bound is "reserved" for the four-dimensional paper is wrong: the principal paper uses it itself (Lemma 3.3). The outline also leaves out stationarity and the mutual-information comparison.
- Section 6 (the companion) matches the paper on the points checked: Lemma 2.15 (the weighted plank input), Proposition 6.8 (degree restoration), the cap ceiling $N_0^{-Su+o(1)}$ and the three regimes. The table's descriptions "thin planar sheets" and "$q^2/J_c \sim N_0^{-\ell}$" were not checked.
- Section 7 gives a Nikodym-set definition that differs from the paper's (the paper uses points with a unit geodesic segment meeting the set in proportion at least $\lambda$, for $\lambda$ near 1), and adds $S^4$ and $\mathbb{H}^4$ as examples. The curved-Kakeya bullet omits the hypotheses (fixed chart, nondegenerate phase, Bourgain's condition). The three-dimensional consequence is about compact sets containing whole quadratic curves $(w + (tI_2 + t^2B)y,\ t)$ under rank conditions, not "unit line segments sweeping along quadratic curves".
- Section 8 dates both preprints 24 September (the principal paper is dated 23 September). Its "human/AI analytic arguments" and "currently undergoing rigorous evaluation by the global scientific community" are unverified. There is a typo, "compactness,0-measurability".
