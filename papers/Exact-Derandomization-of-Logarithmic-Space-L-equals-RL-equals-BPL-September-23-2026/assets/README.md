## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the paper:

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**

Clear errors:
- Slide 6 is titled a timeline of "space complexity in graph connectivity", and its closing line says the goal is log space "for directed connectivity". Both are wrong. The 1983, 1999 and 2021 bounds on the slide are bounds for BPL, randomized log space. Directed connectivity is the complete problem for NL, and the paper does not put it in L (slide 15 correctly lists L versus NL as open). Hoza's bound is garbled as O(log(1.5 n/√(log log n))); it is O(log^{3/2} n / √(log log n)). Calling Borodin–Cook–Pippenger the "first deterministic bound" ignores Savitch (1970).
- Slide 7:
  - The left panel says a PRG "shrink[s] a long random string into a short seed". A PRG does the opposite: it stretches a short seed into a long string.
  - The right panel says "No random bits required for the core calculation". That is wrong. The paper's estimator uses a random environment of O(log n) bits, and determinism comes from running it on every environment and taking the median.
  - The matrix of fractions and percentages and the panel of formulas (det(M), an eigenvalue, ∫P(x)dx) are decorative and depict nothing in the paper.
- Slide 9 labels E the "reward / correction bucket" and says the mass is deposited "into the reward buckets (E)". In the paper E is the correction matrix and the reward is the vector W, which becomes W + EW. The identity on the slide is correct.
- Slide 11 attributes the control of active nodes to "a strict ordered-support counting argument" and to weights being "drained into the Reward rapidly". In the paper the ordered-support argument gives the factorial truncation bound (Lemma 4.1). The at-most-doubling of active vertices comes from counting the columns that detectors flag (Proposition 4.5): few columns can have many substantial entries. The doubling conclusion itself is correct.
- Slide 15 glosses P versus BPP as "Does randomness help when memory is unlimited?". P versus BPP is about polynomial *time*. The L versus NL line is correct.

Smaller issues:
- Slide 1's subtitle says the paper "proved" L = RL = BPL. It is an unreviewed preprint's claim. The labels "high entropy" and "zero entropy" are decorative.
- Slide 3's takeaway gives the reason for the time bound as preventing "infinite coin-flipping loops". The reason that matters here: without a time bound, one-sided-error randomized log space equals NL. The table itself is correct.
- Slide 4's graph is schematic and reuses the labels T1, S1, S2 inconsistently.
- Slide 5's probability 28/64 = 7/16 is correct. The tree and the three rows of dots are decorative and do not show the 64 coin sequences or which 28 accept. "Precisely calculate" overstates what is needed: the paper approximates to within an inverse polynomial.
- Slide 8 calls p₀ "the exact acceptance probability". p₀ is the vector of acceptance probabilities from every configuration; the machine's acceptance probability is its entry p₀(x₀).
- Slide 10 prints "Parent Node" twice and draws four clones; the paper uses a fixed number D₀ of copies. Its "problem" line is a loose paraphrase: copies are used for the large entries in columns where the detector switches correction off.
- Slide 12:
  - It has the typo "memory memory".
  - "The original transition matrix is entirely drained" overstates. A residual remains, bounded by |V₀| 2^{−(H−1)L}.
  - "Step 3: Filter active nodes" is not a separate step: the detectors run inside each stage, before the copy split.
- Slide 13 says "the hierarchy produces a massive family of estimates". The family comes from the randomized estimator, one estimate per environment. "Accurate on more than 75%" means of the environments that retain the start vertex. "The errors are perfectly discarded" overstates: the median is accurate because more than three quarters of the values are.
- Slide 14 has the typo "logic logic". It phrases the test as "if the result is ≤ 11/24, it was a NO input". The paper's direction is the converse: a no input gives at most 11/24, a yes input gives at least 13/24, and the algorithm compares with 1/2.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- Section 1 announces "three primary architectural components" and lists four.
- Section 2 (history table) has several wrong dates:
  - Borodin–Cook–Pippenger: 1989 should be 1983.
  - Nisan–Szemerédi–Wigderson: 1999 should be 1992.
  - Cohen–Doron–Sberlo–Ta-Shma: 2021 should be 2023.
  - Chen–Cohen–Doron–Khaskelberg–Ta-Shma: 2023 should be 2026.
  - Cheng–Wu: 2023 should be 2026.
- Section 2 also adds two claims the paper does not make:
  - Saks–Zhou's "time bound remains exponential/super-polynomial".
  - Reingold's algorithm "relies on zigzag graph products".
- Section 3:
  - The Theorem 1.2 summary claims that unary input "forces q = O(log n)". That is false. A unary q can be as large as the input length, and the paper says the running time is exponential in general q. Only q = O(log n) gives polynomial time.
  - "Theorem 12.1 & Corollary 12.3" merges two results. Theorem 12.1 is the fixed-accuracy approximation, Corollary 12.3 is PromiseBPL = PromiseL, and RL = L is part of Theorem 1.1.
  - Corollary 12.4 is stated with c > 0; the paper allows c ≥ 0. It also omits that on other inputs the machine outputs an accepting computation or a failure symbol.
- Section 4 says the question was "posed by Gill (1977) and Aleliunas et al. (1979)". The paper credits Aleliunas, Karp, Lipton, Lovász and Rackoff with asking it explicitly. Gill developed the complexity theory of probabilistic machines.
- Section 5:
  - Step 4 calls SL_H(ℤ) ⋉ ℤ^H a "Lie group"; it is a discrete group. Shalom's results are for SL_H(ℤ) and the pair (SL₂(ℤ) ⋉ ℤ², ℤ²). The paper derives the affine gap from them (Lemma 6.1).
  - Step 5's eighth-moment formula has X_f(v) f where the paper has X_f(v)/f. The conditional bound has N/(f π_f) where the paper has N f/π_f (Lemma 12.2).
  - The box "Mathematical rationale for the 8th moment" is NotebookLM's own explanation; the paper fixes s = 8 without giving that argument.
  - Step 6a's carry-propagation rationale for signed digits is also NotebookLM's. The paper's stated reason is that redundancy lets consecutive finite approximations define an exact stream even when they use different truncations.
- Section 6 cites "Rabin (1981)", which is not in the paper's bibliography; the paper cites Schwartz (1980) for polynomial fingerprints. It also dates Shalom to 2000 (it is 1999) and Avizienis to 1961 (it is 1963).
- Section 7 says the proof also applies to "restricted constant-visit random tapes". Constant-visit tapes are the model of Cheng and Wu's prior work. The paper handles fresh bits, and Section 13.1 converts a one-way random tape whose head may pause on the current bit.
- The rest of the report matches the paper: the statements of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, Lemma 2.1, the correction identity and factorial truncation, Propositions 4.5 and 4.7, Lemmas 6.3 and 9.9, the signed-digit alphabet, the memory-allowance bound and the median step.
