## Errata

NotebookLM is good at structure and visuals but sometimes invents or garbles mathematical detail. Known issues, found by reading each output against the papers:

**`infographic-overview.png`**
- The 2D bar chart labels the disk "$\pi^3\approx 9.87$". It should be $\pi^2\approx 9.87$. The square's bar also carries a stray "0.0" label; its value is 8.
- The "Volume Product" panel calls it "an affine invariant quantity". For origin-symmetric bodies, where the polar is taken about the origin, the invariance is under invertible *linear* maps (the paper's equation (3) in Section 2). Affine invariance belongs to the non-symmetric version with the Santaló point.
- In the Hanner panel, "Cortesian Products" should be "Cartesian products" and "Conves Joins" should be "convex joins". The joins are $\ell_1$ sums, but the label reads "$\ell$, Sums", and the $\ell_\infty$ subscript on the products is malformed.
- The lens in "The Proof Engine" is drawn as a rounded ellipse with latitude lines. The paper's lens narrows to two tips at $\pm i$, where its half-width $\lambda(t)$ vanishes, and only its *boundary* heights are uniform (Lemma 3.1). Treat it as a schematic.
- "Proof avoids flows or induction" comes from the generation prompt. The lower-bound argument indeed uses no flow and no induction on dimension, but Lemma 2.2 (the Hanner volume product) is proved by induction over the recursive construction.
- The Hansen–Lima box says that having metric medians is "equivalent to 3-ball intersection". The paper attributes that equivalence to Lima's criterion and proves only the direction it needs (Lemma 8.2). The Hansen–Lima theorem itself (Theorem 8.3) starts from the 3.2 intersection property. It also says such spaces are "linearly isometric to Hanner polytopes"; the precise statement is that the space is built from the real line by $\ell_1$ and $\ell_\infty$ sums, so its *unit ball* is a Hanner polytope.
- "Proofs fully formalized in Lean 4" (and the "Lean 4 Formalized" badge) overstates the scope. [`lean/docs/087.md`](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/087.md) lists the symmetric inequality and the equality classification as formalized and says the functional inequalities are not included. It is self-contradictory about the general case, and the catalogue's review status is `unchecked`. See [section 7 of the explainer](../README.md#7-what-it-does-not-prove-and-caveats).

**`infographic-history-timeline.png`**
- Many typos: "aeross" and "sentury" in the context box ("across", "century"), "Solid balis and elipsoids", "Maĥler", "planar 2B case" (2D), "Characterize Bail Intersections" and "three-hall intersection property" (ball), "origin-synnoetric convex hodies" (origin-symmetric convex bodies).
- The Mahler card is headed "Poses the General Problem". In this family "general" means the *non-symmetric* problem. Mahler's 1939 transference paper posed the symmetric problem in higher dimensions; the card's own text ("solves the planar case and poses the conjecture for all higher dimensions") is closer.
- The context box says the minimum problem has driven "over a century" of progress. That is wrong: Mahler posed it in 1938–39, about 88 years before the 2026 preprints.
- The context box says the minimum problem has driven progress across "geometry, analysis, and computer science". Computer science is not mentioned in the papers. **Unverified.**
- The 1981 card says Hansen and Lima "establish the three-ball intersection property characterising normed spaces". They did not introduce the property. They classified the finite-dimensional normed spaces that have it (iterated $\ell_1$ and $\ell_\infty$ sums of lines).
- The 1985–1988 card credits "Reisner, Meyer & Gordon" with classifying the equality cases among unconditional bodies. That classification is Meyer (1986) and Reisner (1987); Gordon's contribution, with Meyer and Reisner, is the 1988 short proof of the zonoid inequality.
- The pictures (the 1956 star polytope, the zonoid, the linking curves) are decorative, not mathematical diagrams.

**`slides.pdf` / `slides.pptx`**
- **Slide 2:** the "Convexity" example is a bean shape with an inward dent, so it is *not* convex. A segment between two points near the dent would leave the shape.
- **Slide 3:** the heading "Translation Matrix" is meaningless here: no translation and no matrix is involved, just a list of polar pairs. The boxed formula $K^\circ=\lbrace y\mid x\cdot y\le1\rbrace$ omits "for every $x\in K$", which the caption supplies.
- **Slide 4:** the value 8 is labelled "Minimizers (Conjectured)". In the plane the minimum 8 was *proved* by Mahler. The open case before this paper was dimension 4 and up.
- **Slide 6:** "Mahler conjectured that all of these achieve the absolute minimum" is anachronistic. Hanner introduced these polytopes in 1956. Mahler's conjectured value, $4^n/n!$, is that of the cube and its polar.
- **Slide 7:** the closing quote ("An open problem for nearly a century, cracked by an AI-generated proof") states as fact what the slide's own 2026 entry correctly calls a claim. The papers are unreviewed preprints.
- **Slide 10:** the lens is drawn rotated by 90°, with its tips on the left and right and vertical hatching. In the paper the tips are at $\pm i$ (top and bottom), and the uniformly distributed coordinate is the *vertical* (imaginary) one. The text box gets this right. Compare the hand-made [lens figure](figures/lens.png).
- **Slide 11:** the directions of the two bounds are right (analytic lower bound $S\ge1$, geometric upper bound). "Their total missing volume dictates the maximum bound" is garbled, though: the upper bound holds because the simplices sit inside the polar, and the missing volume is the slack in that bound. The inset is a copy of the paper's Figure 2, with a garbled caption reading $\Sigma_X=\mathrm{conv}\lbrace\pm b_1,\pm b_5\rbrace$ instead of $\mathrm{conv}\lbrace\pm b_1,\pm b_2\rbrace$. The line "CRITICAL: Never swap these bounds" is an instruction from the generation prompt that leaked onto the slide.
- **Slide 12:** "The geometry forces a unique metric median structure" is wrong. The paper says explicitly that "the median need not be unique". The slide also equates medians with the three-ball property; in the paper, medians *imply* that property (Lemma 8.2). The lifted body $K\oplus_1[-1,1]$ is a double cone over $K$, not the blob drawn. The lift does not by itself force the missing volume to vanish: because the lift is still an equality case, the missing volume of its polytope approximations tends to zero.
- **Slide 13:** the subtitle "bypassing a century of expected techniques" overstates the age of the problem (posed 1938–39). The "What the proof does not use" column repeats the generation prompt. Its claim that Steiner symmetrization "belongs to Santaló's maximum proof" came into the prompt from the Wikipedia source and is **unverified** against the papers. On "induction on dimension", see the note under the overview infographic. "The Gromov width of $K\times K^\circ$ is exactly 4" needs $n\ge2$; the symplectic companion states it for the interiors $\mathrm{int}\ K\times\mathrm{int}\ K^\circ$.
- No revision has been made, so all of the above ships as generated.

**`beginner-explainer-report.md`**
- **Wrong focus.** The prompt asked for the symmetric paper's proof, but the report treats *The Mahler Conjecture for General Convex Bodies* as "the primary preprint" and the symmetric paper as "companion [24]". Its section 5 walks through the general companion's cone, Laplace and Gaussian-projection proof, not the lens, probability-sum and simplex argument of this paper. For the symmetric proof, read [section 5 of the explainer](../README.md#5-the-main-idea-of-the-proof). The technical constants in that section (0.80, 1.8, −0.14 and so on) were not checked against the general paper.
- The "cube sanity check" is wrong. It says the cone over the cube is the non-negative orthant, but in the general paper's reduction the orthant corresponds to a *simplex*; the cube's cone has cube cross-sections. It then mixes in "simplices tiling the cross-polytope", which belongs to the symmetric paper's argument. In that argument, the check is that $S=1$ and the simplices $\mathrm{conv}(0,\pm e_i)$ tile the cross-polytope.
- Step 8 says the symmetric companion reaches Hanner bodies by the same cone-splitting rigidity. It does not: the symmetric paper uses a lift, metric medians, the three-ball property and Hansen–Lima. "Absence of smooth minimizers … dual cones split into one-dimensional factors" likewise describes only the general case.
- **History table dates.** These are wrong according to the papers' bibliographies: Bourgain–Milman "1986" (1987), Kuperberg "2000" (2008), Nazarov "2008" (2012), Meyer–Reisner shadow systems "1988, 1990" (2006), Kim–Reisner "2014" (2011). "Mathieu Meyer (1986) completed full planar equality characterization" mixes up two papers. Meyer 1986 concerns unconditional bodies, and the planar non-symmetric equality case (triangles) is Meyer 1991. The planar symmetric equality cases are parallelograms, not only squares (Reisner). Mastrantonis–Rubinstein is cited by the general paper as 2024, not "2021". Mahler's planar polygon paper is dated 1938 in that bibliography.
- Iriyeh–Shibata's method is described as "topological deformations of minimal surfaces". The papers say nothing like this. **Unverified**, and probably invented.
- Names: "Nikolaos Mastrantonis" should be Vlassis Mastrantonis, and "Masanori Shibata" should be Masataka Shibata.
- Section 4 claims consequences that neither paper states: strengthening "dual Sobolev, Prékopa–Leindler, and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities", "Gaussian measure concentration", and "sharp transference bounds for dual lattices … successive minima". **Unverified.**
- Section 7 calls the symmetric results "fully formalized". The scope document lists them as formalized, with the functional results excluded and the catalogue review status `unchecked`. The report does record the `unchecked` status correctly.
